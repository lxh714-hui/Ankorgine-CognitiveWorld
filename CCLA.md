## 2. Ownership and copyright license

The Entity retains its ownership. Only to the extent it actually owns or has
authority to license relevant rights, it grants the Steward a worldwide,
perpetual, irrevocable, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to use, reproduce,
modify, prepare derivative works of, publicly display, publicly perform,
distribute and otherwise exploit covered Contributions, and sublicense those
rights through multiple tiers, including under AGPL-3.0-only and separate future
commercial or proprietary licenses.

This is not an assignment or a claim that all employee work belongs to an
employer. Check ownership under applicable law and actual employment, contractor
and commissioning agreements. Employee-owned, joint-owned and third-party work
requires the actual holders' appropriate authority. The Entity cannot grant
rights it lacks. Applicable notices and third-party obligations remain binding.
No acceptance, support or commercial release is promised.

## 3. Patent grant and termination

For patent claims the Entity owns or controls and can license, necessarily
infringed by a Contribution alone or combined with CognitiveWorld as it existed
when submitted, it grants the Steward and recipients a worldwide, perpetual,
non-exclusive, royalty-free patent license to make, have made, use, sell, offer
for sale, import and otherwise transfer that Contribution and combination. The
Steward may pass this license through permitted distribution/sublicensing chains.
Unrelated patents and subsequent combinations are not included merely here.

If a beneficiary initiates patent litigation, including a counterclaim or
crossclaim, alleging infringement by the Contribution or submitted combination,
this agreement's patent license to that beneficiary ends when the claim is filed.
Independent public-license rights remain governed by their own terms; this clause
does not purport to revoke them.

## 4. Designated people and individual CLA relationship

Maintain a signed schedule of designated contributors, relationship to the Entity,
authorized scope and dates. Designation is not ownership evidence. Removal affects
future designated scope, not grants already validly made.

An individual CLA may cover personal rights or assurances; the CCLA covers only
rights the Entity can authorize. Neither automatically substitutes for the other.
Before merge, verify the applicable agreements and resolve missing or overlapping
authority. A person need not claim employer-owned rights, and the Entity must not
claim employee-owned rights it does not hold.

## 5. Assurances and disclosure

The Entity represents that its signatory and grants are authorized. Disclose the
rights basis, necessary consents, third-party sources/licenses/notices, known
relevant patent claims, confidentiality obligations and material restrictions.
Clearly exclude work not authorized for the proposed grant.

Disclose substantive AI assistance, known tool/model, affected material and review
of origins, rights, correctness and safety. AI content is not presumed original or
licensable. Do not publish confidential prompts or personal data. Notify the
Steward of discovered material errors.

Except for express assurances, Contributions are supplied as is, without
performance, fitness, support or unknown-rights warranties to the extent law
permits. No unlimited indemnity obligation is imposed by this draft.

## 6. Preserved rights and execution

Commercial relicensing applies only to rights validly granted. It never revokes
existing AGPL rights; compliant commercial competition is allowed. No trademark
license or non-compete restriction is added to the code license.

This draft has no effect until final legal review, completion and verified
execution by the authorized representative and Steward. Governing law, forum,
successor/assignment terms and non-waivable rights require agreement before
execution. Later edits do not rewrite executed agreements.
