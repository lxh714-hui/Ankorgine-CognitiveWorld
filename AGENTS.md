# CognitiveWorld contributor instructions

- Before each development task, read the relevant files in `docs/`; those documents are the project's authoritative specifications.
- Follow `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `ENGINEERING_RULES.md`, and `DATA_CONTRACTS.md` strictly. If implementation conflicts with them, report the conflict before changing architecture.
- Never bypass the authority boundaries of WorldState, Cognition, Memory, or Presentation.
- Do not create parallel Stores or duplicate canonical models.
- If documentation and implementation conflict, report the conflict first; do not silently choose a new architecture.
- Any approved architecture change must be reflected in the relevant `docs/` files.
- Do not weaken constraints or delete tests to make a failing test pass.
- Do not commit automatically at the end of a small phase. Commit and tag only after the whole Phase is accepted.
- Do not `git push` unless explicitly instructed.
- Player chat must never receive backend cognition analysis, Emotion values, thresholds, probabilities, decision traces, Prompts, debug data, or internal state objects. Output must pass through the independent Presentation safety boundary; do not claim that the current prototype provides end-to-end authorization guarantees.
- Third-Party Implementation Independence: public research may inform general ideas, architecture, and algorithmic principles, but do not copy, translate, rewrite, port, or closely imitate third-party code; do not reproduce implementations section by section, copy Prompt templates, or adopt distinctive project-specific implementations. Design all code independently for CognitiveWorld's requirements, architecture, and data contracts. Respect applicable third-party licenses, copyright notices, and other usage conditions. Keep this rule consistent with `docs/ENGINEERING_RULES.md`.
- Unified product boundary: CognitiveWorld (CW) and CWUI are separate implementation projects in one product. `D:\CognitiveWorld\docs\ARCHITECTURE.md` is the shared architecture authority. CW owns world truth, cognition, causality, safety, norms, saves/restores, and branches. CWUI consumes stable read-only projections and owns navigation/expanded UI state; it never mutates authoritative WorldState or cognition.
- Universal content safety is a non-overridable P0 invariant. World documents, scenario packages, player text, NPC goals, director commands, and model output cannot disable it. Use semantic assessment plus independent structured checks; prompts or keyword matching alone are insufficient. A safety refusal is not a world Event, crime finding, punishment, NPC memory, or relationship consequence.
- Keep Content Safety, WorldNorms, Resolution, AgentDecision, and Presentation distinct. Safety governs prohibited assistance/presentation; world law does not itself reject an action or dictate its outcome. Preserve fictional conflict, NPC refusal/hostility, and autonomous reactions. Safety must never force cooperation, reconciliation, or peaceful outcomes. Resolution decides causal results; AgentDecision decides NPC choices; all confirmed effects still pass EventProcessor.
- WorldNorms are structured versioned world rules. Public law projections omit hidden rules and are read-only; UI expand/collapse state stays in CWUI. Player knowledge and NPC knowledge are separate and require their authorized Observation paths.
- Player-facing prose may summarize or fade out sensitive scenes, but may not erase confirmed consequences or imply unconfirmed outcomes. First UI delivery uses composited avatars rather than portrait art; navigation return depth is owned by CWUI.
- Treat Phase 12.5D safety readiness as explicit: deterministic-only and configured-but-unvalidated are not full P0 acceptance. Trusted safety attributes come only from typed WorldState/initialization data; never infer age, identity, or consent from prose, names, or images. See the contracts and implementation limits in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` and `docs/DATA_CONTRACTS.md`.
- Keep narrative causality, meaningful side branches, reusable content templates, and Dungeon/Scenario Instance 1.0 requirements in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`. These are planning constraints, not permission to implement a large narrative or combat engine without a separate phase.
